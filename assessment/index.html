NetAcad course completion

You're enrolled in three Cisco Networking Academy courses: Linux Essentials, Python Essentials 2 and DevNet Associate. The self-enrolment links are in Week 1, under Pre-class preparation. Use your @siswa.um.edu.my email so your progress can be matched to you.

This component is marked on completing the courses. Their modules are also the pre-class preparation for several weeks, so keeping up with them each week does double duty.

The alternative assessment

At half of your total mark, this is the most important single assessment. Expect to explain how mechanisms work, compare two approaches and justify a choice, and predict how something will fail — not to recall syntax or list facts. Memorising slides won't be enough; understanding will. Comparing and designing are where Weeks 3, 8 and 9 in particular are assessed.

The best preparation is the work you're already doing: the self-assessment checklist at the end of each week, the quick checks in the lectures, and your lab write-ups.

Your lab portfolio

Submit each lab on the Spectrum LMS, under that week's lab. Your work goes in a GitHub repository containing:

  • a README that explains what you did and why;
  • the scripts and files another student would need to run it again.

On Spectrum, submit either:

  • the link to your repository; or
  • the link to a site you've published from it with GitHub Pages — your write-up as a web page, with the outputs and diagrams that support it, and a link back to the repository.

A published site is optional, but it's a good way to make your write-up easy to read, and to build a portfolio you can show beyond this course. Whichever you submit, make sure it's viewable by the link, and check that it holds no passwords or tokens.

Reproducibility is part of the grade: if someone else can't re-run your work from your repository, it isn't finished.

From Week 2 on, every lab runs on your own addresses, worked out from the last digits of your student ID (each lab explains how). Your outputs should show those addresses: that's part of how your work is recognisably yours, so if you work with a partner, each of you runs the lab with your own numbers.
